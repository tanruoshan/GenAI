\todo{P2, first sentence after results: which method wins, at which levels, and whether a crossing survives on
valid-only LLM scores.} Three differences between the methods can explain this pattern. First, the two methods use
the garbled letters differently: BERT never sees them as input, only through the reranking term and one slot at a
time (Sec.~\ref{sec:models}), whereas the LLM reads all garbled forms at once and can use one to interpret another.

words hard to fill \citep{shen2020blank}, and our search stops at three pieces per slot, so the 3.5\% of fact slots
that need more cannot be restored at all. Third, the two methods differ in supervision and scale: BERT is fine-tuned
on in-domain text, the LLM has [N] times more parameters but sees only three examples. [If the contamination probe

% ---- P4: generality and use for archives (caveats are result-independent) ----
\todo{P4, first sentence after results: the practical threshold, e.g.\ ``names and numbers restored beyond
[LEVEL] should not be trusted without a check''.} This threshold holds under our conditions: character noise
calibrated on real but mild OCR errors, a damaged region that is known in advance, and newspaper pages legible
enough to have been transcribed \citep{booth-etal-2024-bln600}. On real damaged pages, the threshold is more likely
to be lower than higher. Whatever the method, an archive should not replace damaged text silently. Repaired words
should stay marked, so that a reader can still see where the evidence was damaged; Ithaca's pairing of model
predictions with historians' judgement shows one way to do this \citep{assael2022restoring}.

% ---- P5: conclusion and future work (result-independent) ----
\todo{P5, one-sentence summary of the answer after results.} Three extensions follow directly from our assumptions.
First, repair should be combined with detecting the damaged region, which we took as given. Second, the results
should be checked on real OCR errors from damaged pages rather than on calibrated synthetic noise. Third, repair
could draw on the scanned image itself, whether through image-level degradation as a test bed
\citep{hamdi2020assessing} or through vision-capable models that read the damaged page directly
\citep[cf.][]{kanerva2025ocr}.
