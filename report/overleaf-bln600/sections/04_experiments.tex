\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
% OPTIONAL (recommended): split FRR into visible vs dropped fact slots. Check the count "68 of 692": the test split
%   has ~1,830 fact slots over all levels (150 x sum of Table 2 means), so 692 must refer to something else.
We score the repaired span, not the whole sentence, since only the span was touched. BERTScore F1
\citep{zhang2020bertscore} compares the repaired span with the gold span using \texttt{roberta-large} (layer 17),
rescaled against a baseline so that a score can go below 0; this is our primary meaning-level metric.
\todo{team, SQ3: span-level BERTScore leaves out the context that the SQ3 argument relies on, and at the 1-word
level compares one word with one word. Either (a) add sentence-level BERTScore as a second column, or (b) keep span
level and add: ``Because only the span is compared, context cannot inflate the score; the gap we measure for SQ3 is
therefore a lower bound on what a sentence-level score would show.''} \frr{} is our primary fact-level metric: the
share of gold fact tokens in the span that the repair restores exactly in their slot, punctuation ignored. Because
the share of facts among damaged words falls as the span grows (Table~\ref{tab:damage}), we also report recovery of
the anchor alone, which is the same word at every level and so gives a clean paired series for SQ1, and we split
\frr{} into fact slots with visible letters and dropped slots, which the methods must fill from context alone.
\todo{team: confirm both can be computed from existing predictions.}

As secondary metrics we report exact-word match (the share of all span words restored exactly), span CER pooled per
level (all character edits divided by all gold characters of that level), repair gain (pooled CER before minus
after repair),\footnote{We pool CER per level rather than averaging it per row, because on a one-word span a single
wrong or over-long word can already give a row CER of 1 or more, so short spans would dominate the mean.} the LLM's
format-failure rate and its scores over valid answers only (Sec.~\ref{sec:models}), and a contamination probe. The
probe is a simplified guided completion \citep{golchin2024time,sainz2023contamination}: the LLM receives the first
half of each clean test sentence and is asked to continue it, and a continuation whose similarity to the true
remainder is at least 0.9 counts as near-verbatim. \todo{team: name the similarity measure; say whether the prompt
names \bln{}.} Unlike \citet{golchin2024time}, the probe has no control condition without the dataset name, so a
formulaic phrase can pass the threshold by chance; we therefore read a low rate as reassuring and inspect any hit by
hand.

BERTScore and \frr{} are reported side by side because they can disagree by design (SQ3): a repair that substitutes
a wrong but plausible name can still score well on meaning while scoring zero on \frr{}, which is exactly the gap we
want to measure.
