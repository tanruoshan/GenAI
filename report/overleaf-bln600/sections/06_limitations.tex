\emph{Damage.} Our damage is synthetic character noise in one contiguous span, not a model of torn, stained or
faded pages: intensity is drawn per word rather than spatially correlated as in a real torn patch, and the
confusion table comes from mild OCR errors on aligned words, so it shows which characters get confused but likely
understates how heavy real damage is. Synthetic and natural noise can differ enough that results on one do not carry
over to the other \citep{belinkov2018synthetic}. \bln{} itself excludes pages too faded or damaged to transcribe \citep{booth-etal-2024-bln600}.
\emph{Task.} The damaged region and the number of damaged words are given, so no detection step is tested. Damage is
centred on a fact by design, so facts make up 100\% of damaged words at the 1-word level but only 17\% at 75\%, and
what else falls in the span, a stock phrase or a rare name, is not controlled. \emph{Measurement.} Fact tokens come
from a surface rule and are scored by exact match, so a correct spelling variant (\emph{M'Donald} for
\emph{McDonald}) counts as a failure. With 150 test sentences, only 26 in the longest band, and one noise draw per
sentence, we report trends rather than powered effects. \emph{Comparison.} The methods differ beyond the repair
step: the LLM and BART read the garbled letters directly, BERT only through its candidates and reranking score; BERT predicts a word's
pieces in parallel \citep{shen2020blank} and cannot produce a word part of more than three pieces; supervision, scale and
pretraining differ; and the LLM may have seen \bln{}, which has been public since 2024. Format failures count as no
repair, and the article variant uses gold context and is therefore an upper bound. BART is trained on the same damage
generator as the test data, so its scores are optimistic, and each fine-tuned model comes from one training run
with one seed. The contamination
probe covers only 116 of the 150 test sentences. All data are English
London crime reports, mostly from two weeklies (572 of 600 excerpts), and we test one LLM.
