\subsection{Task and Data}
Given a sentence whose damaged span is known and whose garbled letters are visible, the task is to predict one word
per damaged position; the number of damaged words is therefore also given. All repair methods take the same input
and are scored by the same code (Sec.~\ref{sec:slot}). We build this task on \bln{} \citep{booth-etal-2024-bln600},
600 excerpts of London crime reporting from British newspapers of 1834--1894, each with its source image, OCR output
and a gold transcription. Its gold text, aligned with real OCR, lets us calibrate
our noise on real errors, and prior post-OCR work uses it \citep{thomas2024leveraging}. We split excerpts into sentences with \texttt{pysbd} and pool sentences of 20--60 words
with at least two \emph{fact tokens}: a number, any word containing a digit or pound sign, or a capitalised word that
is not the first word of the sentence (a short stop list excludes titles such as Mr, Mrs, The). This is a surface
rule, not a named-entity tagger: it catches ``Old'' in ``Old Bailey'', misses a lower-cased fact, and flags dates
only through capitalisation or digits.
% (removed 2026-09-29: optional todo, manual precision on 100 sampled fact tokens; not done before submission)
 The pool holds 3,123 sentences from 592 excerpts. From it we draw, with a fixed
seed, 150 test and 20 dev sentences from 157 excerpts (at most three per excerpt, no excerpt in both splits,
balanced across three sentence-length bands); dev is used only for tuning and is never scored as a result.

\subsection{Damage Simulation}
\label{sec:damage}
For each sentence we draw one anchor among its fact tokens -- always damaged, never dropped -- and damage one
contiguous span of $k$ words that centers on it, with $k$ set so the span covers 1 word, then 10\%, 25\%, 50\% or
75\% of the sentence (half-up rounding). Every larger span centers on the same anchor and fully contains every
smaller one (Figure~\ref{fig:spans}), so a higher damage level adds more damaged words around a fixed center; it
does not make any single word more damaged; the per-word alteration intensity below is drawn the same way
regardless of the span size. Each damaged word may be dropped outright (except the anchor). A surviving word has 20--30\% (at least one) of its
letters and digits altered, punctuation untouched, split between deletion and substitution as in
Table~\ref{tab:damage-ops} (the substitution source is a fixed lookup order -- confusion table, then
capitalization, then a look-alike map, then a random character -- not a random draw between them). 
% REVIEW (2): A median per-word CER of 0.25 is only plausible over MISRECOGNISED words (with a 7% mean CER, the
%   median over all words would be ~0). Please state this, e.g. "... per-word character error rate among
%   misrecognised words ...".
The 20--30\% band was chosen to sit around the median per-word character error rate we measured on 29 held-out \bln{} excerpts
(0.25, IQR 0.17--0.40). 
% REVIEW (1): "All calibration" conflicts with Table 2: 14.3% of substitutions (capitalization, look-alike, random)
%   and the 0.10 word drop are not calibrated from data. Suggest: "The confusion table and the per-word rate come
%   from real OCR errors on aligned words, so the noise resembles what OCR output already contains."
The confusion table (85.7\% of substitutions) and this band come from real OCR errors on aligned words; the drop rate and fallbacks are our choice.

Table~\ref{tab:damage} shows the result on the test split: sentence-level CER grows roughly in step with real \bln{} OCR error rates, but
the share of damaged words that are themselves facts falls from 100\% at the 1-word level to 17\% at 75\%. About one in ten fact slots is dropped outright, leaving no visible letters for that fact at all.

\subsection{Shared Slot Input}
\label{sec:slot}
All methods see the same \emph{slot view}: the sentence's undamaged context is left untouched, and each damaged
gold word is replaced by a slot showing its garbled form (a dropped word becomes an empty slot, written
$\langle ?\rangle$). This view is built from the gold tokens and the per-word edit operations, never from the raw
corrupted text, so it cannot leak an easier version of the task. A predicted word is spliced back inside the
original word's edge punctuation, so context words are never rewritten and every difference in score comes from
what a method puts in the slots. For example, the damaged sentence ``A POWERFULLY-BUILT man,
$\langle$same$\rangle$ $\langle$Bbonias$\rangle$ $\langle$Huicl,$\rangle$ $\langle$e$\rangle$ commission agent,
\ldots{}'' has the gold continuation ``named Thomas Hurd, a''; the garbled forms are visible to both repair methods,
but the gold words are not. Showing garbled rather than blanked letters keeps this an OCR-repair task rather than
blind text infilling. Treating the damaged region as known follows the usual split of post-OCR work into detection
and correction \citep{nguyen2021survey,rigaud2019icdar} and the known-position setting of text restoration
\citep{assael2019restoring,lazar2021filling}; it isolates repair from detection, which we leave to future work.

\subsection{Repair Methods}
\label{sec:models}
\begin{sloppypar}
\textbf{(a) BERT + reranking.} We fine-tune \texttt{bert-base-cased} \citep{devlin2019bert} (110M parameters; cased
because fact tokens are partly defined by capitalisation) on clean gold text from the 443 excerpts outside the
sample (2,939 sentences, of which those from 10\% of the excerpts are held out for validation, with zero excerpt or sentence
overlap with test or dev, which we verified directly). Training mirrors inference: for each target word, span words
before it are left as gold text, the target is masked with its true number of subword pieces, and later span words
each become one \texttt{[MASK]}. Fine-tuning settings are in Table~\ref{tab:settings}; the best checkpoint
(epoch 2) lowered the per-piece validation loss from 5.61 (pretrained) to 4.14, and training loss kept falling while
validation loss rose again over the next two epochs, confirming the early stop. At inference, slots are filled left
to right, following the beam decoding of \citet{lazar2021filling} with the settings in Table~\ref{tab:settings}. A slot with a visible
internal hyphen or apostrophe is split into sub-slots filled the same way; only 0.9\% of slots (3.5\% of fact slots)
still need more than three pieces. Each candidate is scored as its mean log-probability per piece plus
$\lambda(1-d)$, where $d$ is the normalised Levenshtein distance to the garbled form, so a fluent completion is
pulled back toward what the damaged letters actually show. This is a direct, modern instance of ``context beats
confusion'' \citep{evershed2014correcting}. We chose $\lambda = 8$ from a grid over $\{1,2,4,8,16\}$ on dev, where it
gave the best exact-word rate at a point where Fact Recovery Rate had already plateaued. Dropped slots, which have no
garbled letters to match against, are scored on the language-model term alone. We keep this variant without list words as
an ablation (\emph{BERT, no dict.}).
\end{sloppypar}

\textbf{(b) BERT + dictionary.} Reranking cannot choose a word that BERT never proposes: on dev, BERT's top
candidates for \emph{Wrighb} (gold \emph{Wright}) were \emph{Smith}, \emph{White} and \emph{andooll}. Our main MLM
method therefore adds, for each slot, the five entries of a word list that are closest in spelling to the garbled
form to BERT's candidates, and scores them with the same formula. The list holds the 16,469 distinct words of the
gold text of the 443 excerpts outside the sample, so no dev or test text enters it. On a dev grid over list size
(5, 10, 20, 50) and $\lambda$ (1 to 128) we chose 5 entries and $\lambda = 64$ by the same rule as above. At this
weight the letters decide most slots, and BERT's context breaks near ties and fills dropped slots.

\textbf{(c) LLM few-shot.} We prompt Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 \citep{yang2025qwen3}, a mixture-of-experts model
with 30B parameters of which 3B are active per token, served through GWDG's SAIA API. The prompt holds a system
message, a task instruction, five solved dev examples (one per level, held out from all scoring), and the same slot
view as BERT with numbered slots, and asks for a JSON list of exactly $k$ strings at temperature 0. We first used
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct \citep{grattafiori2024llama3}, but its dev format-failure rates (21\% and 27\% for the two prompt variants) were above the 15\% limit
we had set in advance for switching model. We use only open-weight models on academic infrastructure, so that the
licensed \bln{} text (CC BY-NC-ND) is never sent to a commercial third-party service. The system message was necessary: without it, an early dev check found all 15 answers in the wrong format, mostly
Python or tool-call code, in line with the finding that stating the input type and output format helps
LLM post-correction \citep{boros2024postcorrection}. An answer of the wrong length, not valid JSON, or cut off by the
token limit counts as a \emph{format failure} and is scored as if no repair had been made, with no retry and no
manual correction. Because output formatting alone can swing LLM post-correction results
\citep{kanerva2025ocr}, we also report the LLM's scores over valid answers only, which separates failing to repair
from failing to follow the format. A secondary variant supplies the whole surrounding article as context instead of
just the sentence; its other sentences are gold, not damaged, so it is an upper bound rather than a fair comparison
with BERT, and we compare it only against the LLM's own few-shot run. Zero-shot prompting was tried on dev and
dropped, so this design cannot isolate how much of the LLM's performance comes from the five examples rather than
the instruction alone. The two methods also differ in supervision by design: BERT is fine-tuned on in-domain text,
the LLM sees five examples. SQ2 therefore compares two practical repair set-ups, not two architectures under equal
training.

\textbf{(d) Dictionary lookup.} To ask whether GenAI beats a spell checker, each garbled word is replaced by the
closest entry of the same word list (Levenshtein distance, ties by frequency); a dropped slot stays empty. The
lookup uses letters only and no context, the pure ``confusion'' side of \citet{evershed2014correcting}. Its limit is
coverage: on dev, the list holds 95\% of damaged words but only 77\% of damaged fact words.

\textbf{(e) BART denoiser (descriptive).} \texttt{bart-base} (140M) \citep{lewis2020bart}, the model family that
\citet{thomas2024leveraging} compared on \bln{}, reads the slot view and writes the sentence back with one word per
slot. We fine-tune it on the BERT training sentences, damaged by our own generator at all five levels with new
damage in each epoch (best epoch 8 of 10). Unlike the other methods, BART is thus trained on exactly the noise
process of the test data, so its scores are optimistic. It was added after the hypotheses were set, and we report it
descriptively only.

\textbf{(f) No repair.} The damaged text itself is scored as if it were a method, giving the floor every repair has
to beat. Table~\ref{tab:settings} summarises the settings.
